Writing Chinese characters is hard. It's also beautiful and satisfying!

JuziGenius™ helps you master one skill: writing Chinese characters from memory. Reading Chinese characters is one thing, but recalling how to write it stroke-by-stroke is another. Most apps focus on reading, so we focus on handwriting.

Instead of boring flashcards, you'll write real sentences. Start with a few characters and unlock more as you go. Every new sentence only uses characters you already know, so you're always building on solid ground.

Use JuziGenius alongside your favorite study tools for grammar and reading — think of it as your cozy corner for putting pen to paper.

As for the name? It's a little pun. In Chinese, jùzi (句子) means "sentence" and júzi (橘子) means "orange." Isn't that genius? Juzi Genius!

The basic loop: you get shown an English sentence, and you write the Chinese for it, character by character, on a drawing canvas — no autocomplete, no traced outlines. The app checks your stroke order and direction as you go.

How you get material to practice: you start with a small set of characters you pick when you sign up. From there you grow your pool by pasting in Chinese text you're reading elsewhere, letting the app suggest common words or characters you don't know yet, or pulling sentences from its built-in library of roughly 17,000 example sentences.

The scheduling brain (spaced repetition): every character you unlock is tracked individually. Each time you write it, the app grades how well you did — perfectly, versus needing hints, versus getting it wrong — and uses that to decide when you should see it again: nail it and the gap grows (tomorrow, then a week, then weeks); struggle and it comes right back today. It's the classic "show me things right before I'd forget them" trick, applied per character.

Why sentences instead of flashcards: the app only ever gives you a sentence if you already know every character in it, and it leans toward sentences that contain characters you're due to review — so you're not writing random sentences, you're getting a workout on exactly what you're about to forget.

Words are their own thing too: two-character-plus words (like 喜欢, "to like") get tracked on their own separate schedule from individual characters, so you can specifically drill vocabulary, not just isolated characters.

Your banks: everything you've ever unlocked or completed sits permanently in Character Bank / Word Bank / Sentence Bank — a running record of what you actually know, separate from whatever happens to be in your practice queue right this second.

What you control in Settings: which of the three (characters/words/sentences) get mixed into your sessions, and how many brand-new characters get introduced per day, so a big paste doesn't dump hundreds of new characters on you at once.

That's really it: unlock characters → get graded on how well you write them → get resurfaced right before you'd forget → naturally graduate from single characters to full sentences as your pool grows.
